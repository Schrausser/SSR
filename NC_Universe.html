The universe

The understanding of the scale of the universe expanded through a sequence of overlapping methods used to measure increasingly distant objects.

1. Measuring Earth ...

  • Distance to the Moon. Aristarchus of Samos (c. 270 BC)1 used the geometry of lunar eclipses to estimate the Moon’s distance, c.f. Heath (1913).
  • Earth’s Circumference. Eratosthenes (c. 240 BC)2 compared the angle of shadows to calculate Earth's size, c.f. Bowen and Todd (2004).

2. and the Solar System

  • Diapason. The universe has, according to Plinius Secundus (79 AD, 1250) and Pythagoras, the size of the Saturn System, c.f. Schedel, 1493.
  • Ptolemy. Based on the Planetary Hypotheses (s. Goldstein, 1967) from Ptolemey, Velho's (1568), universe has the size of the inner Solar System to Mars.
  • The Telescope. Hans Lipperhey, 16083.
  • Relative Planetary Scale. Johannes Kepler (1609) used Tycho Brahe’s observations to prove planetary orbits are linked to its relative distance from the Sun.
  • The Astronomical Unit. Giovanni Cassini (1672, 1684) using Mars parallax (and later the transit of Venus 1761 - 1769, Hornsby, 1771) established the first accurate absolute distance to the Sun.

3. Reaching the Stars

  • Stellar Parallax. Friedrich Bessel (1838, 1839) measured the apparent shift of star 61 Cygni, proving stars are vastly distant suns.
  • Standard Candles. Henrietta Swan Leavitt (Leavitt & Pickering, 1912) discovered the period-luminosity relationship in Cepheid variable stars, to determine a star's true brightness from its pulsation rate and to calculate the luminosity distance.

4. Galaxies and the universe

  • Mapping the Galaxy. Harlow Shapley (Shapley & Curtis, 1921) used globular clusters and variable stars to measure the size of the Milky Way.
  • Beyond the Milky Way. Edwin Hubble (1925) identified Cepheid variables inside Andromeda, proving it was an entirely separate galaxy far outside.
  • Cosmic Expansion. Combining distance measurements with galactic redshifts, Hubble (1929) demonstrated that galaxies are moving away (Hubble's Law), proving the universe is expanding.

5. The expanding universe

  • The Cosmic Microwave Background. Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson (1965) detected the thermal echo of the Big Bang, to date the universe's beginning.
  • Dark Energy and Acceleration. Observations of distant Type Ia supernovae revealed that the expansion of the universe is accelerating, c.f. Riess et al. (1998), Perlmutter et al. (1999).
  • Precision Cosmology. Data from the Planck satellite, gravitational wave detectors, and the James Webb Space Telescope refined the age of the observable universe to 13.8 Ga and its radius to 14.25 Gpc, s. Planck Collaboration et al. (2014).

1: On the Sizes and Distances of the Sun and Moon (Περὶ μεγεθῶν καὶ ἀποστημάτων [ἡλίου καὶ σελήνης]).
2: Eratosthenes’ original treatise, On the Measurement of the Earth (Περὶ τῆς ἀναμετρήσεως τῆς γῆς), is lost. Its contents and methods are known primarily through surviving summaries by later ancient authors, most notably Cleomedes.
3: The first printed publication to announce the invention of the telescope was an anonymous French diplomatic newsletter issued in October 1608 titled Ambassades du Roy de Siam envoyé à l'Excellence du Prince Maurice, arrivé à La Haye le 10. Septemb. 1608. Primarily covering a Siamese diplomatic mission and peace talks with Spain, the final pages report on a "poor, simple, and God-fearing" spectacle-maker from Middelburg (Hans Lipperhey) demonstrating an optical tube that allowed observers atop a tower in The Hague to clearly see the clock of Delft and the windows of Leiden's church miles away.
