# The *universe*

The understanding of the scale of the universe expanded through a *sequence of overlapping methods* used to measure *increasingly distant* objects.

### 1. Measuring *Earth* ... 

- **Distance to the Moon**. *Aristarchus of Samos* (c. 270 BC)[^1] used the geometry of lunar eclipses to <ins>estimate</ins> the <ins>*Moon’s distance*</ins>,  c.f. Heath ([1913](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139854719)).
- **Earth’s Circumference**. *Eratosthenes* (c. 240 BC)[^2] compared the angle of shadows  to calculate <ins>***Earth's size***</ins>, c.f. Bowen and Todd ([2004](https://doi.org/10.1525/california/9780520233256.001.0001)).

### 2. and the *Solar System*

- **Diapason**. The universe has, according to *Plinius Secundus* (79 AD, [1250](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b550140045/)) and Pythagoras, the <ins>*size of*</ins> the <ins>*Saturn System*</ins>, c.f. Schedel, [1493](https://daten.digitale-sammlungen.de/~db/0003/bsb00034024/images/index.html?id=00034024).  
- **Ptolemy**. Based on the *Planetary Hypotheses* (s. Goldstein, [1967](https://doi.org/10.2307/1006040)) from *Ptolemey*, *Velho's* ([1568](http://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb416166390)), universe has the <ins>*size of*</ins> the <ins>*inner Solar System*</ins> to Mars.
- **The Telescope**. *Hans Lipperhey*, 1608[^3].
- **Relative Planetary Scale**. *Johannes Kepler* ([1609](https://doi.org/10.5479/sil.126675.39088002685477)) used *Tycho Brahe’s* observations to prove <ins>planetary</ins> orbits are  linked to its <ins>*relative distance*</ins> from the Sun.
- **The Astronomical Unit**. *Giovanni Cassini* (1672, [1684](https://doi.org/10.3931/e-rara-7549)) using Mars parallax (and later the transit of Venus  1761 - 1769, Hornsby, [1771](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstl.1771.0054)) established the first accurate <ins>*absolute distance to* ***the Sun***</ins>.

### 3. Reaching the *Stars*

- **Stellar Parallax**. *Friedrich Bessel* (1838, [1839](https://doi.org/10.1002/asna.18390160502)) measured the apparent shift of star 61 Cygni, proving <ins>*stars are vastly distant*</ins> suns.
- **Standard Candles**. *Henrietta Swan Leavitt* (Leavitt & Pickering, [1912](https://scixplorer.org/abs/1912HarCi.173....1L/abstract)) discovered the period-luminosity relationship in <ins>*Cepheid*</ins> variable stars, to determine a star's true brightness from its pulsation rate and to calculate the <ins>*luminosity* distance</ins>.

### 4. *Galaxies* and the *universe*

- **Mapping the Galaxy**. *Harlow Shapley* (Shapley & Curtis, [1921](https://archive.org/details/scaleofuniverse00shap)) used globular clusters and variable stars to measure the <ins>*size of the Milky Way*</ins>.
- **Beyond the Milky Way**. *Edwin Hubble* ([1925](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1925Obs....48..139H)) identified *Cepheid* variables inside <ins>Andromeda</ins>, proving it was an entirely <ins>*separate galaxy*</ins> far outside.
- **Cosmic Expansion**. Combining distance measurements with galactic <ins>*redshifts*</ins>, *Hubble* ([1929](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.15.3.168)) demonstrated that galaxies are moving away (*Hubble's Law*), proving <ins>***the universe***</ins> is expanding.

### 5. The *<ins>expanding</ins> universe*

- **The Cosmic Microwave Background**. *Arno Penzias* and *Robert Wilson* ([1965](https://doi.org/10.1086/148307)) detected the thermal echo of the *Big Bang*, to <ins>*date the universe's*</ins> beginning.
- **Dark Energy and Acceleration**. Observations of distant Type Ia supernovae revealed that the expansion of the <ins>*universe is accelerating*</ins>, c.f. Riess et al. ([1998](https://doi.org/10.1086/300499)), Perlmutter et al. ([1999](https://doi.org/10.1086/307221)).
- **Precision Cosmology**. Data from the *Planck* satellite, gravitational wave detectors, and the *James Webb* Space Telescope <ins>*refined the age*</ins> of the *observable* universe to 13.8 Ga and its radius to **14.25 Gpc**, s. Planck Collaboration et al. ([2014](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201321591)).

[^1]: *On the Sizes and Distances of the Sun and Moon* (*Περὶ μεγεθῶν καὶ ἀποστημάτων [ἡλίου καὶ σελήνης]*).  
[^2]: *Eratosthenes’* original treatise, *On the Measurement of the Earth* (*Περὶ τῆς ἀναμετρήσεως τῆς γῆς*), is lost. Its contents and methods are known primarily through surviving summaries by later ancient authors, most notably *Cleomedes*.  
[^3]: The first printed publication to announce the invention of the telescope was an anonymous French diplomatic newsletter issued in October 1608 titled *Ambassades du Roy de Siam envoyé à l'Excellence du Prince Maurice, arrivé à La Haye le 10. Septemb. 1608*. Primarily covering a Siamese diplomatic mission and peace talks with Spain, the final pages report on a "poor, simple, and God-fearing" spectacle-maker from Middelburg (*Hans Lipperhey*) demonstrating an optical tube that allowed observers atop a tower in The Hague to clearly see the clock of Delft and the windows of Leiden's church miles away.
